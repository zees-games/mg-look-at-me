# Design

Why mg-look-at-me is built the way it is. [`../CLAUDE.md`](../CLAUDE.md) is the short version; the horror pass (the witch, the sound, the light, the signs) is [horror.md](horror.md).

## Decision 1: doors are per player, and that is an exclusion list

The brief: everybody does every level themselves, which is what makes a server that never ends. So a door is one solid `StaticBody3D` in the world, and a player who has opened it has its RID in their controller's collision exclusions (`DotFpsController.body.exclude`, rebuilt by `LmGame._apply_doors`). It is open for them and shut for everybody else, and nothing about the level changes. A witch's line of sight to a player ignores the doors THAT player opened (`_clear_between`), because for them it is open. `shared_progress` (off) copies every take, open and use to everybody on the level. `headless_run` walks two players at one door: the one who opened it is 6 m through, the other stops at it.

## Decision 2: a witch's walk is a pure function of time

mg-wipeout's idea again: a witch walks a loop of points (room centres, doorways, points in a room) at a speed, and `LmLevel.witch_pose(index, seconds)` is where she is and which way she and her head face. Every machine poses every witch from the clock, so nothing about her will ever need sending, and the server's "can she see you" and the client's picture of her cannot disagree. She does not chase; she sees. She stops at each doorway for `witch_door_seconds` to open the door (a pause is more time on the loop, so the walk is still a pure function of time; the door swings open for everybody and closes two seconds after she has gone through). She also listens: a run, a door or a use is heard within `witch_hear_range`, a lit flashlight is seen at `witch_light_range` by a witch looking its way, and after `witch_reaction_seconds` her head turns toward it at `witch_turn_rate` and holds for `witch_alert_seconds` (`LmGame._listen`; per player on the server, so a connected client draws her head from the clock). Her feet never leave the loop. Witches never walk into a start room: start rooms are safe, and the lobby is level 1's.

## Decision 3: being seen is a meter

Each tick, every witch on a player's level asks: in range (`sight`, × `flashlight_sight` with the light on), inside her cone (`cone`, turning with her head), nothing solid between. The rate is `exposure_rate` × nearness, × `flashlight_exposure`, × `crouch_exposure`, × `still_exposure`; a second witch adds 35%. Out of sight for `decay_delay` it drains at `decay_rate`. Full is caught: `caught_seconds` of a red screen, then the level's start room, with the level lost under `caught_penalty` 1 (the default) or kept under 0. Her sight is drawn on the floor as a faint red fan, because a meter a careful player cannot see coming is a lottery.

## Decision 4: levels are generated, and proven

`tools/build_levels.py` grows each level's rooms as a tree on a grid, on one to three floors, from a start room (the lobby is 24 m square, the biggest room in the game), and locks every door into a new room behind a puzzle whose parts it puts in rooms already reachable: so every level is finishable by construction, and `solve()` plays the logic and grows the level again until it passes. Later levels have more rooms (14 + n), put the parts in older rooms, lock some parts in chests, and send more witches (2 + (n-1)/3, up to 12), faster (1.5 + 0.05n m/s), sharper (8 + 0.35n m, cone 70 + 1.2n°). Each level also has its events: lights that die, a door that slams behind you, her seen at the end of a corridor. `headless_run`'s solver then plays all 32 in the ENGINE — standing in front of each thing and pressing E through `LmGame.interact`, the events firing as they do — so a document the game reads differently from the builder fails there. The shapes, floors, puzzles and events are [levels.md](levels.md).

## Decision 5: movement is predicted, and so are the doors

Movement is mg-deathrun's: an `LmNetCommand` a tick into dot-net's input buffer, each packet carrying the last five (`INPUT_COPIES`, so a lost packet costs no tick), the owner predicting with the same controller the server simulates. Everything else is mg-dangerous-delivery's: a few JSON events and requests. **The doors are why this game's prediction is its own.** A door open only for one player must be open in that player's PREDICTING controller too, or the client predicts a wall the server walks them through and every snapshot drags them back; so PROGRESS (the owner's holds, opened, used, taken, and the events that have happened to them) goes to the owner alone and is applied to the client's own controller before its next predicted tick. `headless_net` walks a client through a door open only for it and ends 5 mm from the server. The levels go one per LEVEL message after the HELLO, deflated and paced; see "The house on the wire" below. Witches are never sent (Decision 2); `headless_net` checks both ends pose all of them alike from the clock. A chat line beginning with a command the house knows (`/r`, `/l3`) is answered by the house (`LmModule._on_say_requested`), the rest goes to the chat router.

**Furniture** (Kenney's Furniture Kit, 21 models, ×2.3) lines the walls, themed by room name (`LmLevel.FURNITURE`, `ROOM_THEMES`), placed from `LmHash.unit` of level, room, wall and slot so every machine furnishes alike, never within 1.9 m of a door and never deeper than 1.4 m (items are always further in than that, so nothing a level needs is ever behind a sofa). Every piece is a solid StaticBody3D on the world layer, built on a server too: it blocks movement and a witch's sight like a wall, which is what makes it something to hide behind.

### The house on the wire

The 32 levels are about 490 KB of compact JSON. Each goes as its own LEVEL message, deflated (`LmEvents.write_level`, about 90 KB for the house), and the server sends a client its next ones only while what that client has not acknowledged stays under `LmNetBridge.LEVEL_WINDOW` (32 KB); the client acks once a frame (`Ask.LEVELS`, `{serial, got}`, the serial being its HELLO's, so an ack for a house it was sent before a house change moves nothing). The reason is the browser: the engine's web WebSocket peer copies every message the page receives into its inbound buffer (64 KiB, dot-core's `inbound_buffer_exp`) as it arrives and empties it once a frame, dropping whatever does not fit with "Buffer payload full! Dropping data." A burst of the 32 levels lost 5 of them in Chromium, and the client then waits for ever for a house it will never complete; the old 210 KB house lost 6 the same way. A native client reads its socket only when polled, so it never drops, which is why every native suite passed. The server's own send queue has the same 64 KiB limit (`outbound_buffer_exp`) once the kernel's send buffer is full, as it is on a slow link early in a connection; the window bounds that too. **While a peer is being sent a house, everything else told to it is held** and follows the last LEVEL (`_tell`), so a client hears no JOIN, PROGRESS or chat about a house it has not finished, as when the levels went at once. **A READY that arrives before the player is seated is kept** and answered when `add_player` seats them: the client sends READY when its link says PLAYING, which the link decides itself after sending `loaded` on another ENet channel, so with loss the READY can arrive first, and it used to be dropped (the client then waited for ever, on a link that otherwise worked). `headless_net` sends the READY first, then checks the first window, the deflation and that PROGRESS comes after the 32nd level; dot-server-deploy's `lookatme_client` joins a second client over a real WebSocket through a throttled link while the first walks. In headless Chromium (2026-10-13, the shell exported fresh, the server local) the delivered game joined, built the 32 levels, rebuilt the Asylum's 16 on a house change, and the console showed no "Buffer payload full" (181 KB down in 1,372 frames).

### One account, one connection

A player is kept under their account (`key_fn`, `uid:<uid>`), so a reconnect keeps their win. A guest's uid comes from its device id, which a browser keeps per origin, so **two tabs of one guest are one account**. Left alone, the second seat took the first's player under the same key: the first tab's keys then moved nothing while its link stayed up, and its eventual leave took the newcomer's player out too. **The newest connection wins**: `LmNetBridge.add_player`, finding the key held by another peer, takes that peer out exactly as a disconnect does (`remove_peer`, so the other clients see a LEAVE then a JOIN) and emits `superseded`; `LmModule` then kicks that session with `SUPERSEDED_REASON` ("You joined from another tab or window"), which the shell shows as its disconnect sentence. The newcomer starts as a reconnect does. Refusing the new tab instead would strand a player whose old tab is already gone (closed, crashed, a dead link still inside its timeout), and dead input on a live link is the worst of the three. The family has no shared "same account joined again" path: dot-auth's `single_session` refuses a ticket for a second SERVER, not a second connection to this one. `headless_net` seats two peers under one key and checks the first is dropped and its leave spares the newcomer (armed: without the takeover four checks fail); dot-server-deploy's `lookatme_client` connects a second real client as the same guest and checks the first is disconnected with that sentence.

## Decision 6: a house is a map, and the players vote between them

The brief: the base game is a map an owner can replace, with a time limit, through dot-map and dot-vote. A **house** is a directory of level documents with a `house.json` ({kind, id, name}); the built-in one is `levels/` ("The House", 32 levels), `houses/<id>/` holds the others (the Asylum, 16, written by the same builder with its own seed and names), and an owner's own goes in `houses/` or `user://lookatme_houses/`. `LmGame.change_house` replaces every level and puts everybody in the new lobby with their reach reset; the bridge sends each client the house again (HELLO, then one LEVEL each), and the client drops the old house's witches. `LmVote` (mg-deathrun's course vote, re-aimed) puts every house on a dot-vote ballot when `house_minutes` (45) is nearly up, the current one and an extension included, applied at once since there are no rounds; `!rtv` any time; 0 minutes is rock-the-vote only. `lm_house <id>` for an operator. **Winners are per house**: the winners file is `{house: {key: when}}`, a crown is the current house's (getting out of 16 levels is not getting out of 32), and a flat file from before houses is read as the built-in house's. dot-server-deploy's `lookatme_client` checks the delivered server found the Asylum inside its mount.

## Decision 7: a TMC account walks the house as its site avatar

`LmModule._make_identity` is dot-platform's `DotPlatformIdentity` over mg-deathrun's one-slot schema (`LmAvatars`: the site avatar's top picks one of the Kenney people, `from_site`). The bridge seats a player with `avatar_fn` (the platform module's `player_for(session).avatar`, else the stock person for the key), and JOIN carries the avatar DOCUMENT, capped at 1 KB, not the skin it picks, so a slot added later needs no new wire. Admission finishes after the seat, so `player_admitted`, `player_avatar_changed` and `player_renamed` all end in `LmNetBridge.refresh_player`, which sends the same JOIN again; a client that already has the player takes the new name and face (`LmPlayer.set_avatar` rebuilds the figure on the next draw) rather than seating a second one. `headless_net` checks both.

## The HUD's prompt is dot-ui's

"Take the red key" is a `DotKeyPrompt` under the crosshair (`LmClient.prompt`, in the menu's theme: the client hands the HUD's root to dot-menu's `follow_hud`, by name, with `LmClient.piece_theme()` as the accent, the lamp's amber and nothing else, so a theme picked in the menu restyles the prompt and the HUD's lines live and the default is dark; `headless_run` checks it), with the key the `lm_use` action is bound to drawn as a key: `use_key()` binds E only if nothing has bound the action, and the key line at the bottom reads the same binding. It was a Label writing `[E]` with the letter fixed. The pack therefore needs a client shell with dot-ui API level 4; `headless_run` checks the derived requires.json, the prompt and its key, and that the prompt, the full chat log and a ballot do not meet at 1280x720. Look at it with `tools/shot.sh --view=prompt` (`--houses=asylum,house` after a house change and back). A suite reads the HUD only after the client has drawn a frame (`headless_run`'s `_drawn`, two `process_frame`s): one, after a `physics_frame` wait, resumes before the client's `_process` and reads the frame before, which once looked like the prompt vanishing after a house change. A player rebinds it on the menu's Controls page (`bind_use`), and the prompt and the key line say the new key (`headless_run` checks R).

## What running and rendering found

The stories are in [history.md](history.md).

- **dot-player-controller needs dot_player and dot_net linked**; without them `DotFpsController` does not parse and every script that names it fails with it.
- **Level 1's witch was out of reach**: its rooms each hang off the lobby, so a witch started in a dead-end room walked its corners, behind the red door. `patrol()` now starts a witch in a room with a neighbour to walk to; level 1's walks the Workshop and the Servants' Hall, where the red key is.
- **Sound is only provable under xvfb** (`xvfb-run -a godot --path . res://tools/audio_probe.tscn`: ALSA, a real pool player sounding the heartbeat at a 0.9 meter, the click, a drone on each witch in earshot); `--headless` has the Dummy driver, so dot-audio runs its null sink and plays nothing.
- **The lobby was as dark as the house** with one lamp in the middle of a 24 m room. Lit rooms get a lamp every cell and a half.

## Decision 8: sound is a dot-audio catalogue, the catch is a dot-fx flash

`LmAudio` is the family's shape (mg-prop-hunt's `PhAudio`): ids in a `DotAudioCatalogue` naming files in `audio/`, all on the SFX bus, played through a `DotAudioManager` the settings' master and effects volumes are bound to. What each sound is for, and how loud, is in [horror.md](horror.md#sound). The lub-dub's timing stays in `LmAudio._process` (it is the meter, heard). **A drone is asked for every frame** (`LmAudio.witch`), because the manager refuses a sound beyond `max_distance` (18 m) at its start and a drone asked for once from across the level would never sound; drones of a level the player is not on are stopped (they used to hum on from a hidden node).

**Connected, a sound the server decides comes as a SOUND event (an `LmAudio` id, appended to `LmEvents.Kind`) and `LmClient._listen_for_sounds` plays it**: the latch of E is the server's, since only it knows E landed; never a sound played only in the offline branch of `_act`. `headless_net` checks the client plays it. Sounds the client can read off the world it already has (footfalls, a door the owner opened, a witch's steps, the stinger, the shriek) are the client's own, the same offline and connected.

The red screen while caught is the meter's vignette and stays the HUD's. The moment of being caught is a dot-fx `caught` SCREEN flash (red, 0.55, 700 ms) and a `caught_jolt` shake, built in `LmClient._build_effects` and bound to the settings, so "allow flashes", the three-a-second limit and the shake slider apply, plus the witch in your face and her shriek (`LmClient._scare_at`). `headless_run`'s sound section checks every id the client plays is in the catalogue, every file it names is on disk, that the volume settings reach the mixer, and that a catch flashes unless flashes are off.

## Settings, and Escape opens them

`LmSettings` is dot-menu's `DotMenuSettings` with this game's schema: sensitivity (ACCOUNT, bound to the controller's tunables, which both samplers read), field of view (SERVER_CLAMPED, default 75), master and effects volume (bound to `LmAudio`'s mixer), shake and flashes (bound to the client's `DotFxManager`). Escape opens the screen; walking is suspended while it is up. `headless_run`'s "the client's settings are read" boots a real client and presses Escape.

**Every key is an `LmBindings` row read through its action** (Enter opens the command line as a fixed second key), and the HUD's key line is drawn from the table. H is help (dot-menu's stock key; `LmSettings._fill_help` writes the rules), so no row may default to H, and the menu has dot-menu's stock Help and Leave; the same `headless_run` section checks them.

**M is the house list** (dot-menu's map menu): `LmGame.house_rows()` and `change_house()` serve both solo and the server, where dot-vote's map menu lets the owner's `sv_map_menu_flag` open a house now and gives anybody else a vote. No row may default to M; `headless_run` and `dedicated` check it.

**The mouse reaches the look through `LmClient._input`**, which hands each `InputEventMouseMotion` to the sampler in use (the player's offline, the client's connected) while the pointer is captured: a `DotFpsSampler` reads the keys itself but is only handed mouse motion, and until 2026-10-11 nothing handed it any, so the view turned only as the player walked. `headless_run`'s "the mouse turns the view" sends real motion through `Input.parse_input_event`; a check that sets yaw directly cannot catch this.

**Cameras and bodies** (the first play test, family-wide): F5 is the view key under the server's `mp_forceview` (dot-player-controller's `DotPlayerViewRules`, bound REPLICATED by the module and followed through the link; the config's `allow_third_person` false starts it at 1, and an older server's HELLO saying so still holds); `_place_camera` applies it every frame. The third-person camera is swept from the eye by a dot-core `DotCameraProbe` against `LAYER_WORLD`, and a `DotCameraFade` on the camera is handed the other players. `mp_solidplayers` (1) puts `LAYER_PLAYERS` in every player's motor mask through `DotPlayerBodyRules.apply_to`, which also lets two players placed on one spot walk apart (`headless_run`'s door check is that case).
